AI project requests: what to build, what to hand off

Twenty submitted requests, consolidated into nineteen distinct projects and sorted by whether we can actually deliver them. The constraint is not build difficulty. It is data access and who owns the system after launch.

The single unlock

Seven of these requests — late-registration surge, year-over-year section pacing, day and time patterns, department-level FTE projection, dual enrollment tracking, modality trends, and the schedule visualizer — are the same project wearing seven hats. They all run off one dataset we do not currently hold: student-section registration records with dates attached, for the last four fall and four spring terms.

One extract request to Institutional Research covers work that six people submitted independently. The registration date field is the critical piece: we cannot rebuild a velocity curve from census snapshots, because nobody archived daily counts from Fall 2023. But if Colleague retains the registration transaction date on each student-section record, every prior term can be reconstructed retroactively.

Requested fields — term code · course · section · part-of-term · modality · campus · meeting days & times · instructor · hashed student ID · student-type flag (dual enrollment / HS) · registration date · drop date · status · credits

Hashed IDs are sufficient for everything except cohort tracking, and they keep the request FERPA-defensible. Confirm the registration-date field exists before committing to any timeline.

Client-side single-file tools. Data never leaves the browser, which is also the answer when IT asks about governance. Ranked by priority, though #1 should ship first because it has no dependencies.

1

Schedule heatmap — day, time, and modality across the department

Requested by AZ

Feasibility Impact
Pros

Zero dependencies. The schedule is already public, so no IR request, no PII, no governance review. Immediately answers which slots are overloaded and which are empty.

Cons

Shows the current picture, not demand. Pairs best with #3 once historical data lands.

RecommendationBuild this week from a schedule export. Ships in an afternoon and buys credibility for the larger data ask.

2

Registration velocity — how big is the late surge, really?

Raised by email · ANTH-106 example: 10 in summer → ~20 at start

Feasibility Impact
Pros

Directly changes cancellation decisions. Telling a dean that sections at 10 in mid-August historically land near 18 is the difference between holding a section and killing it. Nothing else here has that leverage.

Cons

Entirely dependent on the IR extract. The decision window closes in about three weeks.

RecommendationMost time-sensitive item on the list. Send the data request today, not after the meeting.

3

Year-over-year pacing by section, in the existing dashboard

Raised by email · extends current dashboard

Feasibility Impact
Pros

We already have the dashboard, the scraper, and the pipeline. This is an extension, not a new build. Answers "are we ahead or behind last fall" at the level people actually schedule at.

Cons

Comparisons must be day-matched, not date-matched — the same calendar date sits at different points in the registration cycle year to year.

RecommendationHighest impact-to-effort ratio in the set. Same extract as #2, so they ship together.

4

Course and department-level enrollment projection

Requested by SB

Feasibility Impact
Pros

We see FTE at the college level but not where scheduling decisions get made. Feeds instructional staffing and tutoring demand proactively rather than reactively.

Cons

It is a forecast with real error bars, and small sections will have wide ones. One bad miss discredits the whole tool.

RecommendationBuild it, but present ranges rather than point estimates. Set that expectation with Academic Affairs up front.

5

Modality migration — how fast are we shifting online?

Raised by email

Feasibility Impact
Pros

Same extract, small additional effort. Quantifies a shift everyone senses but nobody has measured — including whether online sections are drawing new students or just relocating existing ones.

Cons

Enrollment data shows that students are migrating. It cannot show why, and it cannot test the "they think it's easier because of AI" hypothesis at all. See the conditional tier.

RecommendationBuild the descriptive half now. Keep it clearly separated from the causal question, which is a different project.

6

Dual enrollment and high school segmentation

Raised by email · GEOG-115 runs ~⅓ high school

Feasibility Impact
Pros

Answers a live worry with a concrete number. HS cohorts register on a different calendar than degree-seeking students, so blended counts actively mislead at this time of year.

Depends on the student-type flag being clean and consistent in the extract. Worth spot-checking before drawing conclusions.

RecommendationCheap add-on to the same dataset. Separating these curves may fully explain the apparent shortfall.

Modality outcomes — are online sections serving students well?

Raised by email · the causal half of #5

Feasibility Impact
Pros

This is the question that actually matters. If we keep adding online sections, we should know whether we are serving students or relocating them. Requires DFW rates, next-course success, and persistence by modality.

Cons

Badly confounded. Students who choose online differ systematically from those who don't, in ways unrelated to modality. A naive side-by-side produces a misleading number that gets quoted in meetings for three years.

RecommendationWorth doing, but with IR as a partner, not alone. Do not promise a fast answer to a slow question.

Five things to say in the room

  1. Most of these aren't AI projects. They're data, reporting, and workflow projects. That's fine — but naming it keeps expectations honest and stops "AI" from becoming the word we use when we mean "someone should fix this."
  2. One data request unlocks seven submissions. Rather than twenty parallel efforts, we're asking IR for one extract. That framing is much easier for everyone to support.
  3. Client-side processing is our governance answer. These tools are static HTML files; the data is processed in the browser and never transmitted. Lead with that the moment student records come up, because they will.
  4. The registration-surge question is urgent, not merely interesting. It's August 11. Section cancellation decisions get made in the next three weeks. This is the one item where a two-week delay costs something real.
  5. I can't be the college's software department. Anything with logins and a database has an owner obligation attached that outlasts the enthusiasm. The redirect tier isn't reluctance — it's making sure those needs reach people who can actually support them.
